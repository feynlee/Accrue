# Push-up: scientific record

Research record behind the push-up exercise guide and its 3D bone-and-muscle animation. Covers the standard push-up and close variants (knee, incline, decline, push-up plus).

**How to read this file**

- `[R#]` points to the numbered reference list at the end. Every reference has authors, year, title and a DOI or URL.
- **[Evidence]**: measured in a peer-reviewed study or systematic review.
- **[Convention]**: coaching convention from a certifying body (NSCA, ACSM, NASM, ACE) or physio text, not tested directly.
- **[Estimate]**: our own geometric estimate, made from body proportions and the measured values around it. Use these for the animation, but do not quote them as findings.
- EMG values are given as %MVIC (percent of a maximal voluntary isometric contraction) unless stated otherwise. Different labs normalize in different ways. Compare values within a study, not across studies. Dynamic peak values above 100% are possible because the reference is an isometric test.

Research date: 2026-10-22.

## 2. Joint kinematics

### 2.1 Range of the elbow and depth

| Item | Value | Type | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Elbow at the bottom (lab standard) | About 90° of flexion | Evidence (protocol) | Borreani 2015 [R27]; Sandhu 2008 [R23] |
| Elbow at the bottom (measured range) | Concentric phase analyzed from 105° to 35° of flexion | Evidence | Suprak 2013 [R7] |
| Depth criterion (test standard) | "Elbows bend to 90°, chest should nearly touch the floor", chest 2 inches (5 cm) from the floor | Evidence (protocol) | PMC9362895 [R45] |
| Depth criterion (coaching) | Chest nearly touches the floor, or elbows at about 90°, whichever comes first | Convention | NASM [R42] |
| Top position | Full elbow extension in test protocols; NASM says avoid hard lockout | Convention (conflicting) | ACE [R43]; NASM [R42] |

### 2.2 Shoulder: elbow flare and elevation

"Elbow flare" here means the angle between the upper arm and the side of the trunk, seen from above.

| Condition | Measured humeral elevation | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Elbows at the sides | 35.5° ± 4.9° | Suprak 2013 [R7] |
| Self-selected | 53.6° ± 1.6° | Suprak 2013 [R7] |
| Elevated ("90°" style) | 64.8° ± 0.7° | Suprak 2013 [R7] |
| Lab standard at the bottom | Shoulder abducted about 45° | Borreani 2015 [R27] |
| Coaching | "Elbows at approximately 45° from your body" | NASM [R42] (Convention) |

Key finding [Evidence]: with the shoulders elevated (about 65°), the scapula did not increase upward rotation and posterior tilt as the elbow extended. The authors linked this to a smaller subacromial space and advised keeping elevation **below 60°** [R7].

Sagittal plane [Estimate]: with the hands under the shoulders and the trunk at 15–20°, the shoulder is in about 70–75° of flexion at the top (90° minus the trunk angle). At the bottom, the elbow sits above and behind the shoulder, so the humerus is in mild extension (about 15–30°) combined with about 45° abduction.

### 2.3 Scapula

Scapular angles across the concentric phase, self-selected arm position, from Suprak 2013 [R7] (Evidence). The sign convention follows the paper: negative tilt is anterior tilt, and negative "external rotation" is internal rotation.

| Elbow flexion | Upward rotation | Tilt (−= anterior) | ER (−= internal rotation) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 105° (bottom) | 19.9° | −17.1° | −44.5° |
| 85° | 19.1° | −16.1° | −48.9° |
| 65° | 17.7° | −15.6° | −52.9° |
| 45° | 15.3° | −15.0° | −57.3° |
| 35° (near top) | 14.1° | −14.7° | −59.5° |

Reading: as the elbow extends, the scapula internally rotates by about 15° (protraction around the chest wall) and downwardly rotates by about 6°. The descent reverses this: the scapula retracts.

**Push-up plus phase** [Evidence, wall push-up plus with bone pins, Lunden 2010 [R8]]: from the start to the plus position, internal rotation goes from 16.9° to 42.8° (+26°), upward rotation goes from 19.6° to 13.9° (−6°), and tilt goes from −3.7° to −7.7° (4° more anterior). The authors call this a position potentially associated with impingement.

**Hand rotation and scapula** [Evidence, Abdollahi 2025 [R9]]: in healthy athletes with the hands turned inward, posterior tilt went from 25.7° to −16.1° during the concentric phase. Athletes with chronic shoulder pain showed much smaller scapular changes.

**Serratus anterior role** [Evidence]: it drives upward rotation, posterior tilt and external rotation of the scapula, and holds the medial border and inferior angle against the chest (anti-winging) [R36]. Normal full arm elevation needs 45–55° upward rotation, 20–40° posterior tilt and 15–35° external rotation [R36]. People with impingement show less upward rotation, more anterior tilt and lower serratus anterior activity [R37].

**Scapular winging** [Evidence]: men with winging had higher pectoralis major activity (79.2 vs 39.7 %RVC) and lower serratus anterior activity (39.8 vs 56.3 %RVC) than controls during push-up plus exercises [R19].

Hand force rises with cadence [Evidence, Yang 2011 [R6]]. Vertical force at the hand, as % body weight:

| Body angle | 3 s / 3 s | 2 s / 2 s | 1 s / 1 s |
|---|---|---|---|
| Decline −10° | 60.1% | 66.5% | 73.1% |
| Standard ≈15° | 40.3% | 45.6% | 53.2% |
| Incline 30° | 30.4% | 33.0% | 40.9% |
| Incline 45° | 18.6% | 21.9% | 29.3% |

The Yang values come from a hand-surface measurement and look like single-hand values, so use them for ranking variants, not as totals.

Other kinetics [Evidence]:

- Elbow axial force averages 45% body weight with normal hands. The elbow flexion torque is 56% of maximal extensor strength with normal hands, 71% with hands together and 29% with hands apart. Hands placed further forward ("superior") raise valgus torque [R31].
- Load across the wrist, elbow and shoulder depends on palm position relative to the shoulder, plane of arm motion, foot position and speed [R32].
- L4–L5 compression in the standard push-up is 1838 N. It is about 2300–2840 N with balls under the hands and 5848 N in the one-arm push-up [R25].

Pectoralis heads [Evidence, Kang 2014 [R20]]: the standard push-up favors the sternal head. A flexed trunk favors the clavicular head and serratus anterior. Marcolin 2015 recorded both heads; both rose in the narrow-base variant [R13].

### 4.2 By phase

- Concentric (pushing up) activity is much higher than eccentric (lowering) [Evidence, Sandhu 2008 [R23], stable surface]: triceps 179 vs 68, pectoralis major 165 vs 51, upper trapezius 94 vs 16, serratus anterior 45 vs 30 (%MVIC).
- The lowering phase showed the least activity in all muscles, with smaller phase differences in the abdominals and erector spinae, which work as stabilizers [Evidence, Marcolin 2015 [R13]].
- Push-up plus phase: serratus anterior reaches up to 123% MVIC with an upper trapezius/serratus ratio below 0.2 [Evidence, Ludewig 2004 [R18]].
- In the push-up plus, lower trapezius rises from 19% to 24.8% MVC from 70° of elbow extension to the plus phase. Serratus anterior is 45–57% MVC. Infraspinatus peaks at 30% below 50° of elbow flexion [Evidence, San Juan 2015 [R5]].

### 4.3 What changes the muscle activity

**Hand width** [Evidence, Kim 2016 [R11]], normalized to shoulder width (100):

| Muscle | 50% width | 100% | 150% width |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pectoralis major | 115 | 100 | 92 |
| Triceps | 117 | 100 | 86 |
| Serratus anterior | 71 | 100 | 119 |
| Pectoralis minor | 132 | 100 | 84 |
| Infraspinatus | 163 | 100 | 76 |

Narrow hands raise pectoralis major and triceps activity compared with wide hands [Cogley 2005 [R10]; Marcolin 2015 [R13]]. A narrow base is best for triceps and posterior deltoid [Youdas 2010 [R12]].

**Hand position, front to back** [Evidence]:

- Hands placed back (toward the waist) raise pectoralis major and lower triceps activity [Gouvali 2005 [R4]].
- Forward and backward hand placement (±20 cm) give the highest abdominal and back muscle activity; use care with low back pain [Marcolin 2015 [R13]].

**Push-up plus** [Evidence]:

- The standard push-up plus gives the highest serratus anterior activity and lowest upper trapezius/serratus ratio. The UT/SA ratio rises in this order: standard < knee < elbow < wall push-up plus [Ludewig 2004 [R18]; Kang 2019 meta-analysis [R21]].
- Serratus anterior by variant: standard 77, knee 39, wall 28 %RVC [Park 2014 [R19]].
- Push-up plus is among the top serratus anterior exercises [Decker 1999 [R24]].
- Best serratus activation with low upper trapezius: full elbow extension, normal shoulder width, 110–120° shoulder flexion [Kang 2019 [R21]].
- Knee push-up plus with homolateral leg extension raises serratus anterior [Maenhout 2010 [R29]].

**UT/SA ratio by exercise** [Evidence, Martins 2008 [R28]]: wall push-up 0.69, bench press 0.14, push-up 0.39 (stable).

**Unstable surfaces** (results conflict) [Evidence]:

| Finding | Source |
|---|---|
| Meta-analysis: serratus anterior unchanged (+0.01%), upper trapezius slightly different (−2.85%) | Kang 2019 [R21] |
| Serratus anterior 29 → 84–96 on wobble board, disc or dome | Borreani 2015 [R27] |
| Serratus, middle and lower trapezius up; deltoids down (push-up plus) | Torres 2017 [R30] |
| Triceps and rectus abdominis up only with hands on the ball; pectoralis unchanged | Lehman 2006 [R24b] |
| Activation declines in all muscles on unstable bases (static press-ups) | Herrington 2015 [R38] |
| Global EMG: unstable 49.6, standard 38.2, plus 30.1, incline on a stable surface 15.8 | Kowalski 2021 [R22] |
| Pectoralis and triceps higher on Swiss ball, mainly concentric | Sandhu 2008 [R23] |

**Incline and decline EMG** [Evidence]:

- Incline 30°: pectoralis 46, triceps 50 (3 s cadence). Decline −10°: 80 and 81 [Yang 2011 [R6]].
- Hands on a bench: pectoralis 21, triceps 22, rectus abdominis 13 [Lehman 2006 [R24b]].

**Equivalence with the bench press** [Evidence]: at matched EMG, elastic-band push-ups and bench press give similar strength gains [Calatayud 2015 [R41]]. Weighted push-ups and bench press show the same kinematics and muscle activation [van den Tillaar 2019 [R44]].

## 6. Common faults

| Fault | Mechanism and risk | Type | Cue to correct |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sagging hips, lumbar hyperextension | The abdominals give most lumbar stiffness [R26]; sag means the abdominals are not holding the trunk [R42]. A physio source says regress when this appears [R48] | Evidence + Convention | "Squeeze your glutes, ribs down, body like a plank" |
| Piking (hips high) | Shortens the lever and lowers the load; shifts load toward the shoulders | Convention | "Hips in line with shoulders and heels" |
| Elbows flared to 90° | Elevation above about 60° limits scapular upward rotation and posterior tilt and may reduce subacromial space [R7]. Wide hands move elbow torque to the shoulder [R31]. NASM lists flaring as a shoulder-stress fault [R42] | Evidence + Convention | "Elbows about 45° from your sides, like an arrow, not a T" |
| Scapular winging | Low serratus anterior and high pectoralis activity [R19]; serratus holds the scapula to the chest [R36] | Evidence | "Push the floor away; spread your shoulder blades at the top" |
| Shoulder shrug, elevation | High upper trapezius relative to serratus is linked with impingement [R37]; push-up plus lowers the UT/SA ratio [R18] | Evidence | "Shoulders away from your ears" |
| Head drop, forward head | Breaks the straight line | Convention | "Look at the floor just ahead of your hands" |
| Partial range | Less range trains less of the movement; test standards require 90° elbow or chest about 5 cm from the floor [R45] | Convention + test standard | "Chest to a fist's height from the floor" |
| Hands too far forward | Raises elbow valgus torque [R31] and trunk muscle demand [R13] | Evidence | "Hands under your shoulders" |
| Fingers turned inward | Raises posterior and varus shear at the elbow [R33] | Evidence | "Fingers point forward" |
| Wrist pain | Hyperextended wrist loads the dorsal ulnar wrist; neutral wrist is likely safer [R34] | Evidence | "Use fists or handles"; handles do not lower muscle activation [R12] |
| Bending the elbows in the plus phase | Moves load back to pectoralis and triceps and away from serratus [R48] | Convention (physio text) | "Arms straight, just push the floor away" |

## 7. Shoulder health

**Subacromial space** [Evidence]:

- Keep humeral elevation below 60° (self-selected or tucked) for rehab and healthy people [R7].
- The plus phase puts the scapula into internal rotation and downward rotation, a position linked with impingement; use it cautiously early in rehab for people with impingement [R8].
- Retraction widens the subacromial space compared with protraction [R36].
- Scapular dyskinesis is present in most shoulder injuries, and scapular rehab can help [R38b].

**Who should modify** [Evidence + Convention]:

| Group | Modification | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Shoulder impingement or pain | Tuck elbows (below 60°), shorter range; start the plus on a wall with care | [R7], [R8] |
| Scapular winging | Standard push-up plus has the best PM/SA ratio | [R19] |
| Wrist pain or injury | Neutral wrist on fists or handles | [R34], [R35] |
| Elbow pain or prosthesis | Avoid inward-turned hands; avoid very narrow hands (71% extensor torque) | [R33], [R31] |
| Low back pain | Avoid forward/backward hand placement; avoid unstable or one-arm versions | [R13], [R25] |

**Progression ladder by load** (from Section 3):

1. Wall push-up (lowest load; high UT/SA ratio [R18])
2. Incline, hands at 61 cm: 41% body weight [R2]
3. Knee push-up: 49–62% [R1], [R2]
4. Incline, hands at 30.5 cm: 55% [R2]
5. Standard: 64–75% [R1], [R2]
6. Decline, feet at 30.5 cm: 70% [R2]
7. Decline, feet at 61 cm: 74% [R2]
8. Unstable surface, weighted vest, or one arm [R25], [R44]

Knee push-ups load less than incline at 30.5 cm by GRF, but have a larger change from top to bottom [R1].

| Top-plus | 4.0 | 75–80° E (chest rises 2–4 cm) | 35–45° E | 0° [R5] | ≈90° E | Full protraction: IR +10–25° beyond top [R8]; UR −5° [R8]; tilt −4° [R8] | ≈16° E |

Scapular angles use Suprak's convention [R7]: IR is internal rotation (negative "ER" in the paper), UR is upward rotation, and negative tilt is anterior tilt. Lunden's plus-phase changes [R8] come from a wall push-up plus and are applied here as differences, not absolute values.

**Notes for the rig** [Estimate]:

- `rig.js` side view: 0° points down, 90° right, 180° up. At the top, the forearm and upper arm are close to vertical (about 0°). At the bottom, the forearm stays near vertical (convention: forearms vertical at the bottom) and the upper arm points up and back toward the feet.
- Use `scap` retraction about +1 (retracted) at the bottom, 0 at the top, and −1 (protracted) at top-plus.
- Knee push-up: knees on the floor, knee flexion about 90°, hips at 0°, trunk-thigh line straight.
- Incline and decline: tilt the whole body to the box angle and keep the same arm keyframes.

## 9. User-facing cues, ranked

1. **Body in one straight line, head to heels.** Squeeze your glutes and brace. [R26], [R42]
2. **Hands under your shoulders, slightly wider, fingers forward.** [R7], [R33], [R43]
3. **Elbows about 45° from your sides.** Not straight out like a T. [R7], [R27], [R42]
4. **Lower until your chest is a fist's height from the floor.** Elbows at about 90°. [R27], [R45]
5. **Push the floor away.** Let your shoulder blades spread at the top. [R18], [R24]
6. **Shoulders away from your ears.** [R18], [R37]
7. **Lower for 2 seconds, press up in 1–2.** [R46]
8. **Breathe in on the way down, out as you push.** [R47]
9. **Wrists hurt? Use fists or handles.** [R12], [R34]
10. **Too hard? Raise your hands on a bench or drop to your knees.** [R1], [R2]

### Gaps found

- No push-up EMG data was found for the gluteus maximus.
- No study gives absolute wrist-extension or ankle angles during the push-up. Those values are geometric estimates.
- Marcolin 2015 [R13] reports EMG in mV in figures only, so no %MVIC numbers are taken from it.
- Unstable-surface results conflict for the serratus anterior: a large rise in Borreani 2015 [R27], no change in the meta-analysis [R21]. Do not promise a muscle benefit from unstable surfaces.
- Breathing guidance is convention only. No push-up study tests it.
